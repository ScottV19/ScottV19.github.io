excerpt: "It is well known that the supermassive black hole at the center of a galaxy and the galaxy itself are intimately connected. This connection translates for example into a clear relation between the mass of the black hole and that of the bulge. This connection is also evident when these supermassive black holes actively accrete matter and become an Active Galactic Nucleus (AGN). For example, there is a clear relation between the radio-loud AGN incidence and the stellar mass of the host galaxy (e.g. Sabater et al. 2019). \n A recent important discovery from LOFAR radio surveys is that radio jets, produced by accreting supermassive black holes, tend to escape along the minor axis of the galaxy. This indicates that the orientation of the black hole’s spin is linked to the large-scale motion in galaxies. In contrast, the most powerful radio sources are more likely to be associated with massive tri-axial or round galaxies, consistent with a scenario where these objects are linked to galaxies with a merger-rich history (Barišić et al., 2019; Zheng et al. 2023). \n With the latest Euclid data release (Euclid DR1) covering roughly 500 square degrees in the North, we now have a uniform, gravitational weak-lensing quality survey overlapping with the LoTSS DR3 release (Shimwell et al. In prep.). The detailed Euclid imaging will enable the investigation of the morphological properties of AGN host galaxies up to z ≈ 2 with unprecedented quality. Combining this with the large number of radio galaxies from LoTSS DR3 will provide the statistics to enable us to accurately measure the alignment between radio AGN and their optical counterparts for the full radio source population. \n To explore the feasibility of the study described above, we have already performed a test using the Euclid Q1 and LOFAR data in the EDF-N which showed a clearly measurable signal. The next step is to increase the number of radio sources to decrease uncertainties and to allow for the study of correlations between the alignment signal and other properties of radio AGN. With a survey area that is ~20 times larger than the EDF-N the signal will certainly be strong enough to do this. \n In light of our latest state-of-the-art cosmological simulations done in Leiden (Huško et al. 2025), this study could present a great opportunity to both verify and test our understanding of galaxy evolution and its implementation in simulations. Moreover, this study could shed light on the connection between radio jets and their galaxies and help us understand the formation of giant radio galaxies for example (Oei et al. 2024). On a larger scale, we could potentially even test whether there is a significant alignment between host galaxies, radio galaxies and filaments of the cosmic web (e.g. Jung et al. 2025) using the capabilities of Euclid to build a 3D model of the Universe."
