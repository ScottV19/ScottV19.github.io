excerpt: "Radio-loud active galactic nuclei (RLAGN) are thought to regulate galaxy evolution by injecting mechanical energy into their surroundings, quenching star formation, and maintaining quiescence in massive galaxies. While their role in the local Universe is relatively well established, their demographics and evolution across cosmic time remain poorly constrained. A key open question regarding these objects is how the incidence of RLAGN depends on host galaxy properties — such as stellar mass, colour, and luminosity — and how these dependencies evolve with redshift (z). We address this by measuring RLAGN fractions over 0.03 ≤ z ≤ 2.0 using two complementary data sets: a ‘local sample’ from a combination of LoTSS DR2 and SDSS data, and an ‘extended sample’ by combining Euclid Q1 data with deep LOFAR data in the Euclid Deep Field North. Host galaxies are classified using the spectral 4000 Å break strength together with rest-frame (u − r) colours in the local sample and redshift-dependent colour diagnostics in the extended sample, resulting in volume-corrected RLAGN fractions as functions of stellar mass, host colour, and luminosity. At low redshift, we confirm previous results that RLAGN are almost exclusively hosted by massive, red, and quiescent galaxies, consistent with radiatively inefficient, hot-mode accretion. At higher redshifts, the host-galaxy demographics broaden systematically: the contributions from blue and green galaxies increase in comparison to their local counterparts, while the dominance of red hosts becomes less pronounced, suggesting a shift in the dominant host populations. This work provides the first volume-corrected comparison of RLAGN host demographics from z = 0.03 to z = 2.0, confirming the established low-redshift picture while showing that transitional and younger host galaxies increasingly contribute to the RLAGN population at earlier times."
